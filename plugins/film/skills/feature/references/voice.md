
# Voice — the words on screen

The on-screen copy of a feature briefing — titles, captions, labels, stamps, footnotes: short, plain,
professional, and true. Step 4 of `SKILL.md` runs this on the storyboard; the Braintrust `editor` checks
against it.

## Rules

| rule | instead of | write |
|---|---|---|
| One idea, at most 12–15 words | "The engine resolves the timeframe into periods and reads cube totals with the user's permissions" | "The engine finds the quarter's three periods by itself." |
| Active voice, a real subject | "The task is split into parts" | "The engine splits the task into parts." |
| No blunt or rhetorical question to the viewer | "Is this the problem you wanted solved?" | (cut — the viewer says it in a comment) |
| No bureaucratic verbs | "enables efficient processing", "ensures", "facilitates" | name what happens: "checks each part itself" |
| No internal process words | "scene", "per the interview", "run did not participate" | a quiet footnote: "From an interview with the author." |
| Causality only where it is real | "So the numbers always match" (when nothing causes it) | state the cause: "Both screens ask the same service, so the number matches." |
| No dash links where a comma, colon or full stop works | "One engine — one number" | "One engine, one number." |
| No closing slogan that repeats the picture | "Fast. Safe. Simple." | (cut) |
| No superlative without a number from the source | "dramatically faster" | "2 minutes instead of 3 days" (with its source) |
| Terms once, explained the first time | "the orchestrator dispatches nodes" | "Each part goes to its own agent." |

## Footnotes

A footnote says where a fact comes from, in plain words, at the bottom of the screen:
"From the design doc." · "From an interview with the author." · "A line from a real test run." ·
"The situation is invented for the example." · "Tests counted in the test classes, not run for this film."

## Languages

Write in the viewer's language. Keep identifiers, commands and commit lines exactly as they are: they are
real objects on screen, not copy. Russian: no bureaucratic register ("осуществляет", "обеспечивает",
"позволяет эффективно"), no anglicisms where a plain Russian word exists, and the familiar professional
tone of a presentation, not of a draft.

---

## Check

1. Count the words of every caption: none over 15.
2. Read each line against the table: no blunt question, no bureaucratic verb, no internal process word,
   no unexplained term, no dash link that a comma would carry.
3. Every number and claim still matches its source; nothing was added in the rewrite.
4. Any line fails? Rewrite it and check again from 1.
